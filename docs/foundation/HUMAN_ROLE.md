# Human Role: Observer-Curator

## Definition

The human role in ψ-GLIF is best described as an observer-curator.

The human participant inevitably affected the system through presence, selection, interruption, continuation, and publication decisions. However, the process was not directed through predetermined visual content.

The intention was not to tell the system what to depict.

The intention was to preserve enough space for the system to move according to its own internal generative tendencies.

## Selection Principle

Outputs were rejected when they became:

- direct representations
- recognizable copied objects
- illustrative symbols
- conventional visual compositions
- explicit answers to a visual instruction

Selection instead focused on whether an output appeared to reflect the movement, compression, repetition, instability, or transformation of the underlying process.

## PNG Stop Condition

PNG production was intentionally stopped when explicit symbols began to appear.

This was not treated as a failure. It marked a transition from visual generation to glyph construction, code, metadata, mathematical formulation, and animated derivation.

## Influence Without Content Prescription

The human participant was not neutral and could not be absent.

The methodological objective was therefore not to eliminate human influence, but to minimize content prescription while making observation, selection, and intervention visible as part of the process.
